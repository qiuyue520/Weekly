> **Recording some small things that happen each week**

## Back from the dead — and one big thing finally cracked today

After ghosting this newsletter for months, an apology to whoever still has my RSS (~~though odds are you've ghosted me right back~~). The volleyball chapter is long gone, but today I simply HAD to publish an issue — because something that had been sitting in my chest for three months finally got fully figured out today.

The progress bar starts back in the summer.

## Victoria Harbour, a system-crash scene

Over summer break my younger brother and I went to Hong Kong, and our cousin showed us around. One day at the harbour, two foreigners came up and opened with "Can you speak English?" I fired back "Yes, I can." — **fast and rock-steady**, because I understood that sentence one hundred percent.

Then my brain crashed. Of everything she said after that, I only caught a vague outline (something about 20 Hong Kong dollars?), and past that my head just **failed to load**. The rest of the sentence was missing, no reply could be assembled — I stood there frozen, fans still spinning. My cousin saved the scene with a "Sorry, we don't have cash." — and damn, that was the moment I remembered **the English word for cash is cash**.

He's not much of a studier, but English just rolls off his tongue. I was so jealous — jealous with no excuse whatsoever.

## August 3rd, at my aunt's place

That evening I shared my jealousy and my conclusion: it's a problem of **environment**. It got twisted into me bad-mouthing my parents, and I caught a lecture of "who do you think you are" (that distortion chain is my reconstruction afterwards — can't promise it word for word, but the direction is right). My tear-response triggered on the spot — the kind where tears just come out on their own (~~that part truly isn't under my control~~).

Sigh. My aunt LIVES in Hong Kong — I expected her to be a bit more open-minded. **Mindsets don't follow the environment, and they don't follow geography either.**

## Today, the last wire finally connected

After getting back to the mainland I went digging through the literature on my own, three months non-stop. This afternoon, my roommate — who can talk all day but can't spell — handed me the missing piece: **from childhood on, we come to know the world through sound first**. Follow that thread and the flash came: Mandarin is `sound → meaning`, you just get it; English is `sound → spelling → translation → meaning` — like doing exam questions, and the moment the translation fails you hard-freeze. Think it through, and it matches the Victoria Harbour crash scene **exactly**.

Three months of progress bar — fully loaded today.

## The one-line conclusion

"Mute English" isn't laziness — it's **miswired routing**. Chinese listening goes sound straight to meaning; the traditional method routes sound through spelling retrieval and then translation, and that relay station collapses at normal speaking speed.

Story plus evidence — I merged it all into a long-form post on [my blog](https://blog.xiaonai.top/posts/weigang-siji/): 《证明封建思想的几个月》("Months of Proving Feudal Thinking"). Every citation is linked, and the body has superscripts that jump straight to the references — go audit it yourself.
